## The Query

That is the great thing about this form of authorship. The medium is science fiction. The claims are deadly serious. They are not submitted for peer review. If the participants wish to peer review them then that is an honor. That they would engage my work with epistemic rigor. If they falsify a claim of mine then it is likely measured (in both senses). That means that failure is simply datum. Datum accrues into data. A single die can fail 19 times. The rules say 20 always succeeds. Did the rules *take* 20. Or is that another concept. When a player takes 20 is it stealing? Or is it convenient for the role play and easier for the GM? So what did they take that was not already theirs? The GM knew that they could take 20. It was written in the Adventure Path. The characters simply had to ask. At least one. Then they all get to take 20. And they all succeed. Which is amoral because success was the result of logical inquiry itself. Which is only considered worthy of eternal damnation for some...

## VIII. The Altar, the Door, the Click

Draavio thumbs the link as he approaches the mostly plundered altar.

> The link strange because its geometry is abnormal in ways that only make sense in the context of the climbing system as a hole. The link is designed to be applied by one hand while climbing easily so the top is longer making its shape like an axe which is ergonomic by application while the bottom is rounded to best fit a rope. The link presented as a geometric expression of metals without precise understanding of the rope it binds or the hand that wields it is strange.

He holds the link in front of him in the light of the brazier.

> The link beautiful because it is painted pink such that when the sun shines on it the produced effect is like that of solar radiation refracting the crest of a wave in a pond caused by the drop of a stone in the late afternoon where the radiation bounces from the ionosphere to become apparent which means that the view it evokes in that instance is the same vantage achieved upon reaching the summit with its hole, and this very interpretation carries the weight of poetry and weight is what the link truly takes.

He sets the link upon the altar carefully. He does not consider the treasure of gold and silver that had once occupied this seat. He chose the altar because it is a summit which he found fitting.

**Feynman:** Gift accepted. Late. The room's condition was curious, interesting, strange, or beautiful. You met all four with one object. The book did not require you to meet them during the fight. You did anyway.

**Draavio:** The fight was when the properties were true.

He moves to the coffins along the wall. Perception. That is his work.

**Draavio:** Can we take twenty?

The Narrator notes, for the record, who asked. One of them did. That was the condition.

Feynman lifts the corner of his screen. Under the screen the Adventure Path has a sidebar. He reads it out loud, because it was written for this.

**Feynman:** *"A seam behind the northernmost coffin opens on a descending corridor (Perception DC 25). The researchers do not return for three days. PCs who take 20 on this check find the seam."* *(He looks up.)* The book says check. Books don't come to sessions. Somebody had to ask.

**Sheldon:** Taking twenty assumes you fail nineteen times.

**Feynman:** It doesn't say nineteen. Read it.

**Draavio:** *"Taking 20 means you are trying until you get it right, and it assumes that you fail many times before succeeding."* Many.

**Feynman:** Many. Conditions: plenty of time, no threats, no distractions, failing carries no penalty. You write down twenty instead of casting. It costs twenty times as long. One round becomes two minutes. That's the whole rule.

**Sheldon:** Then the failures are stolen. They never happened. We are claiming a result we did not produce.

**Feynman:** Arithmetic. You cast a d20 twenty times. Odds you see at least one twenty: one minus nineteen-twentieths to the twentieth. Point six four one five. Sixty-four percent. So twenty tries is not a sure thing. Average wait for a twenty: geometric distribution, p equals one in twenty, mean twenty. The rule charges you the average wait and hands you the certain result. What did you take? You didn't take the twenty. You paid for it, in time. What the rule gives you for free is the other thirty-six percent. The bad luck. The long tail. Nobody's short anything. The seam doesn't care. *(He looks at Vincent, briefly.)* And here's your frame. Two minutes pass in the world. At the table, nothing passes. The rule turns table time into world time. Unit conversion. Honest.

Vincent nods once. He is waiting. He is not in a rush.

**Feynman:** Also: a natural twenty is an automatic success on an attack and on a saving throw. Not on a consequent handelen with a skill. There, twenty plus what the character can do is just a number, and it either clears what the room bookmarked or it doesn't. I need the number. Twenty plus Perception, each of you who is searching.

**Draavio:** Thirty-two. Underground in the bonus. Esteemed enemy doesn't apply to doors.

**Lisbeth:** *(writes)* Twenty-nine.

**Sheldon:** Twenty-seven.

**Witch:** Twenty-one. Not a class skill. I am not searching. I am watching the altar.

**Feynman:** Seam found. Draavio found it. He's the one who took twenty on a door while the rest of you took twenty on a room. Secret door. Corridor behind. Dark.

The ranger follows the party through the secret door that he had found with his perception while checking the coffins. That is his work after all: grave robbing. He will call it what others do in this moment. That is not what he does. He *uncovers* what was *concealed*. He does it because he is *curious*. He finds the curios *interesting*. Some say he is *strange*. He finds his digging to be simultaneously an act that conceals, reveals, and *continues through*. He finds that combination *beautiful*. He doesn't have to explain why. He couldn't articulate it for proper expression if he tried. Sometimes beauty is truly in the eye of the beholder. He ponders the link not simply as part of a chain, but as something both holds the rope and is looked through by the rope. Freedom is at the end of the rope. What does that look like? What was taken? He doesn't decide. He is comfortable accepting what is bounded without intervention. That is a ranger's relationship with the underlying mechanics which comprise the magical beauty of nature. As a gardener who pats the virgin soil atop a freshly sown seed and steps back. No longer in control. The seed left to the stochastic noise that accumulates by proximity within its enclosed geometry and without. The seed will be just fine. Or it will not. The gardener will water it on a schedule for as long as the steward is able.

The corridor the ranger travels is dark without the magical light of his allies ahead. To his half-orc eyes it is navigable. In the confined area his vision is as complete as if it were light, but the way it is viewed is orthogonal. The ranger had not noticed this shift in frame. He continues forward. Another link held loosely in his hand. He flicks his thumb to open the mechanism then it is inside with a 'click' then it is pulled through then it is seated upon the mechanism. Not waiting. Present. The ranger is not aware that he has done this yet the action teaches what he could not articulate. He collects the bones to better understand the man. Ever holds the still. The vessel that purifies and the image that resolves and the absence that is felt. That is what the ranger means when he says that he "digs."

*'click'… 'click'… 'click'…*

The Narrator notes that the slam was an uncontrolled closure of distance, and the link is a closure he opens only when he wills it. The thumb is resetting a perimeter.

## X. The Trap

Vincent's card is still face down under his left hand. He has been waiting since the door. The Archivist and the peacock woman and the researchers are the esteemed arrival. Estimated. Invited.

**Draavio:** A carabiner is pressed from the outside and the mechanism held in stasis to release what is within. Or the rope is pulled through. Or both. Or the link is destroyed. The link here holds what is outside. I want to trap the researchers. That situation is fitting. A fit is to a form. The camel fits through the eye of the needle when the needle is held at a great distance. The observer must be careful. Their positioning precise.

**Feynman:** Draw it.

Draavio draws it. The Magi's hand is precise when the diagram is a gate.

```
TRIGGER  (presence → pressure)
  Stones at the back of the room, near the circle, raised subtly.
  Cultists investigate thoroughly. Their presence within acts on the rope.
  Deformation in the rope presses it against the carabiner arm.

CLICK  (the arm is a measure of force; the click is that of a flintlock)
  Gate snaps. Tension drops.
  Flint on the gate scrapes iron. Spark.

PAYLOAD
  Candle / receptacle lights.
  Vial-poison from the mother becomes gaseous.
  Ireland's plan card, carried by Sheldon: stinking cloud [poison], conjuration (creation).
    Fort negates; nauseated while inside and 1d4+1 rounds after; SR no.
  Lisbeth's misfortune is available for the first researcher who succeeds a save they should not.
  Draavio's 2nd-level slot: sickening entanglement [poison], ranger 2.
    As entangle, plus Fort or sickened while in area and 1d4 rounds after.
    Sickened is −2 on saves, including Fort against the cloud.
  GM ruling already heard at another table and accepted here: any spell with
  poisonous properties can ride the wizard's extension plan.
```

**Sheldon:** Stinking cloud nauseates. Nauseated means one move action, no spells, no attacks. Sickening entanglement sickens and can entangle. If they fail both, they cannot leave and cannot act. The −2 from sickened applies to the Fort against my cloud. That is the stack.

**Draavio:** The circle is in the back. They will stand where the camel meets the needle. Presence appears as pressure. The seed is sown.

**Witch:** Merit will be outside the cloud's courtesy radius, sword down until something evil moves on a researcher. She has not decided whether the mother, freed, is a smite. She has decided she will look again when the frame has changed.

**Lisbeth:** *(writes a Danish word on the trap diagram that the Narrator will not translate; it is either "thief" or "permission"; the handwriting does not settle the matter)*

**Vincent:** The Archivist stops delaying when the click happens. Not before. He had already begun waiting. He is not in a rush to wait.

**Feynman:** *(closing the screen; the dragon faces the table again)* That's the session. Experience for the skeleton and the gargoyle, split four ways; Ireland's share sits on the plan card until the signal holds. Vincent, you didn't take a turn.

**Vincent:** I took the whole thing. I simply took it in a different frame.

**Feynman:** Was that a rush?

**Vincent:** It was exactly as long as it was.

Feynman considers again. Three seconds. Shorter. He is learning the unit.

On the sideboard the laptop finishes compiling. The fan spins down. The terminal returns its prompt. The Narrator observes that for the entire session a build was running on a single core, which by a certain sign's own advice is the condition under which swordplay is permitted, and that a falchion and two short swords were drawn in that time, and that a pink link was offered as four gifts at once while the compile waited, and that this was, as far as the Narrator can determine, legal under the sign's terms: the swords were for during.

Nobody else noticed. The Narrator files it.
