## A Note on the Preceding Page: Continued (The Proposed Solution)

*Look at the bottom right of this page. There is a number there that does not belong to this page. It belongs to the Merlin page. This is a proposed solution. It has not been agreed upon. The debate follows.*

---

**The Narrator:** The characters had agreed that a crisis existed. They had not agreed on what to do about it. This was, in the Narrator's considerable experience, how most crises proceeded. The Narrator noted that Stanley had not yet moved. The Narrator did not comment on this. The Narrator commented on everything else.

**Feynman:** Here is what I want to know. The operator declared the Merlin page unnumbered. Fine. But does declaring a page unnumbered change its position in the sequence? The page is still the *n*-th page in physical order. It is just not labeled as the *n*-th page. So either pages have intrinsic positions — ordering — in which case the label is cosmetic and this entire crisis is cosmetic. Or pages have positions *assigned* by their labels, in which case an unlabeled page exists outside the sequence and we have a genuine gap. The operator cannot have it both ways.

**McClane:** You're telling me we might have a ghost page.

**Feynman:** I am telling you we might have a ghost page.

**McClane:** I have dealt with ghost pages before. Actually I have not. I have dealt with ghosts in an airport, a building, and once on a bus, but not pages. What number is *this* page?

**The Narrator:** The answer to that question, the Narrator noted, was complicated.

**Cecil:** A listener has submitted a suggestion. The suggestion is as follows: place one page number on the second page of this crisis — this page — at the far bottom right. Place the second page number, the one that should have gone on the Merlin page, on the following page at the far bottom left. This distributes the accounting discrepancy across two pages in a manner that is, if not correct, at least symmetrical. The suggestion was submitted anonymously. The Council reminds you that anonymous submissions are noted and filed and that the filing cabinet is in the dog park, which does not exist.

**Feynman:** Would that actually work? The left-side number on the following page would refer to what, exactly? The Merlin page? This page? The page between the Merlin page and this page that does not exist?

**McClane:** It would work in the sense that both numbers would be physically present in the book, distributed across pages. Whether they would be *right* is a completely different problem that I am not qualified to solve and am not being paid to solve.

**The Narrator:** Stanley thought about this for a moment. The proposed solution had the shape of a solution. This was not the same as being one. But in Stanley's experience, the shape of a solution was often the most that was available.
