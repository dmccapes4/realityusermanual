**The Narrator:** And then they asked the question that made everything considerably worse.

**Feynman:** Does assigning a page number actually number the page? Or is it the physical order — the page's position in the binding — that numbers it, and the printed numeral is only a label we attach to what is already determined? Because if it is the order, then the Merlin page was already page *n* the moment it was bound into the book. The operator did not unnumber it. The operator merely failed to label it. Which means every crisis we have been having is a crisis about a missing label, not a missing number.

**McClane:** *[pause]* You're asking if the page numbers are real.

**Feynman:** I am asking if the page numbers are doing the numbering, or if they are reporting on something that was already there.

**McClane:** In my professional experience, the thing that is labeled is the thing that gets counted. If the page has no number, the page does not exist in the count. This is true of floors, of detonators, and apparently of oak-related interludes in books.

**The Narrator:** The operator — who was also the author — had declared the Merlin page unnumbered without providing a reason. This was noted as irrational. The irrationality was now on record. **McClane:** It is their book.

**Feynman:** That does not make it right.

**McClane:** No. It makes it *their* wrong.
