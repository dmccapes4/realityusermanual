Not adjustable parameters. Structural facts of the planet. The orbital forcing flows through

## III. Beringia — The Primary Valve

**Beringia** is the shallow land bridge between Alaska and Siberia, currently ~50 meters deep
at its shallowest. It has formed and dissolved during each major glacial maximum for at least
the last 500,000 years. [measured: submarine geomorphology, pollen, dating records]

When sea level drops ~50m, Beringia emerges. When it emerges:

Fresh Pacific surface water stops entering the Arctic Ocean. The North Atlantic surface
becomes saltier. Saltier surface water sinks more readily. The Atlantic Meridional Overturning
Circulation — the conveyor belt that moves heat northward — strengthens. The strengthened
AMOC carries more warmth to the North Atlantic margins. The ice sheet edges briefly melt.
Sea level rises. Beringia re-submerges. Fresh Pacific water re-enters the Arctic. AMOC
weakens. The North Atlantic cools. Ice sheets advance. Sea level drops. Beringia re-emerges.

This is not metaphor. This is the measured hysteresis mechanism of the AMOC — confirmed in
fully-coupled global climate model simulations and in the Dansgaard-Oeschger oscillations
recorded in Greenland ice cores. [modelled: GCM simulations; measured: ice core proxy records]

Beringia is not a migration corridor with an incidental effect on climate. Beringia is the
valve that makes abrupt glacial climate transitions possible. Its opening and closing is what
creates the rapid warm-cold swings that appear so discontinuous in the ice core record. The
valve is the mechanism. The migration is incidental to the mechanism.

## IV. Sundaland — Mother Earth's African Climate Thermostat

**Sundaland** is the shallow shelf between mainland Southeast Asia and Australia — the
maritime continent, currently 0–120 meters deep across the Java Sea and the Indonesian
archipelago. During each glacial maximum for the last 650,000 years, the full shelf has
exposed. [measured: paleoclimate records, 450,000-year Sumatran sediment record]

When Sundaland exposes, it closes the Indonesian Throughflow — the ~15 Sverdrup current of
warm Pacific water that normally flows into the Indian Ocean and sustains the Indo-Pacific
Warm Pool, the largest heat engine on Earth.

The chain:

Eastern Indian Ocean cools. The east-west thermal gradient across the equatorial Indian Ocean
intensifies. Stronger equatorial westerlies develop. Cold water upwells along the East African
coast. Subsidence settles over East Africa. East Africa dries.

This is the mechanism that has driven African aridification on 100,000-year cycles. Every
time Mother Earth drops sea level enough to expose Sundaland, East Africa becomes a drier
place. Every interglacial, the shelf re-submerges, the Indo-Pacific Warm Pool rebuilds, and
East Africa receives moisture again. [modelled and measured in the 450K-year Sumatran record;
East African aridification confirmed against the ITF-Walker mechanism by multiple studies]

The Sundaland geocell is Mother Earth's African climate thermostat. The mechanism that drove
human populations south, east, and northward during arid periods — and drew them back during
humid ones — was operated from Indonesia.

## V. Steppe Streams [his, confirmed]

A steppe is not random geography. A steppe is a specific product: dry air descends on the
leeward side of a mountain barrier, depleted of moisture by orographic uplift, and spreads
as a governed exhaust across the continental interior. The steppe is the signature of the
barrier. The barrier is visible in the steppe.

The prediction, stated before the literature check: if the steppe exhaust hits an ocean,
it produces an atmospheric river. If it hits a second mountain barrier, it creates
conditions for condensation and ablation on the downwind face. The steppe is a staging
ground for atmospheric flow, not a passive landscape. Call it the steppe stream.

The literature check confirmed this. The three test cases — the Central Asian steppe
(Tibetan Plateau and Zagros), the South American Patagonian steppe (Andes), and the North
American intermountain basin (Sierra Nevada and Rockies) — each show the same architecture:
mountain uplift, orographic precipitation on windward face, rain shadow exhaust spreading
as steppe, secondary condensation effects on the downwind geological features. The
atmospheric river connection held for the Patagonian case: the Andean steppe exhaust
contributes to the moisture flux that feeds the South American Low-Level Jet, which is
one of the primary sources for the La Plata Basin rainfall. [modelled and measured]

The steppe stream is a component of Mother Earth's atmospheric circulation system, not an
incidental desert. It connects the mountain, the dry interior, and the oceanic or secondary
continental receiver in one coherent governed field.

## VI. The Glacial Cell Reproduction Question [his]

Glaciers calve icebergs. Icebergs drift. We have never observed an iceberg become a new
glacial cell because we are in a warming period and the thresholds are moving the wrong
direction. But the question stands: under what conditions does an iceberg ground, stabilize,
and grow into a new glacial cell?

The sequence the literature describes [modelled: Marine Ice Transgression Hypothesis]:

1. The iceberg reaches a shallow shelf — its submerged draft (~90% of height) must be less
   than the shelf depth.
2. Sea ice forms around the grounded iceberg, reducing lateral calving loss.
3. Snow accumulates on the surface; the grounded ice thickens independently.
4. Mean annual temperature stays well below 0°C — surface ablation cannot overcome
   accumulation.
5. The grounded ice shelf eventually thickens enough to flow.
6. It is now a glacial cell.

The condition Mother Earth must provide: a shallow bank at the right latitude (~65–75°N),
in water cold enough for sea ice formation, in the path of iceberg transport, with mean
annual air temperatures below −5°C.

**Iceland is the best answer she has found so far.** A volcanic hotspot geocell at 65°N on
the North Atlantic ridge, volcanic warming that barely does not prevent glaciation — the ice
cap persists through interglacials. Vatnajökull formed on the volcanic highland. Iceland is
the geocell that enabled the Fennoscandian ice sheet's growth during each glacial inception
for the last several million years.

Mother Earth's reproductive strategy for glacial cells: place a volcanic geocell at the right
latitude. Allow the orbital forcing to drop summer insolation below the threshold. The rest
follows. Not design in the theological sense. Design in the engineering sense: the right
geometry is in position for the forcing to use.

## VII. The Heron Engine [his, mechanism; modelled confirmation pending]

The Heron engine is the mechanism by which a marine ice sheet grows at its base rather than
only at its surface.

The classical picture of glacial growth is accumulation: snow falls, compresses, the ice
sheet thickens from the top down. This is correct but incomplete. The base of a marine
ice sheet — in contact with seawater — is the site of a second growth mechanism.

The ocean near a cold ice shelf is supercooled below the surface freezing point by the
pressure-dependence of the freezing point curve. At depth, the pressure raises the freezing
point of salt water; at the ice-ocean interface, the water is cold enough to freeze onto
the base of the shelf — marine ice accretion. As this accreted ice grows on the underside
of the shelf, it creates a melt plume through differential density: fresh water (less dense)
rises along the ice face, drawing in saltier deep water from below, which arrives warmer.

The asymmetry in this plume — the geometry of the ice face, the buoyancy gradient, the
rotation introduced by the plume circulation — creates a sustained supercooling condition
on the side of the ice face receiving the cold freshwater upwelling. This is the Heron engine:
the melt-plume geometry maintains the supercooling that maintains the basal accretion that
maintains the plume.

A glacier with an active Heron engine grows from below as well as above. The basal accretion
is structurally different from the surface ice: denser, more crystal-organized, less permeable.
It is also invisible from surface observation, which is why it has been underweighted in
models that fit surface accumulation against ice sheet volume.

## VIII. The Bones Are the Receipt

The child's dinosaur was the entry point. The slope was already there. The pump was running.

Australian megafauna went extinct 50,000–46,000 years ago — well before the Last Glacial
Maximum. They did not die in a CO₂ crash. They died in an encounter with humans and fire:
a hunting intelligence the megafauna had not evolved against. The CO₂ restructuring of
Australian vegetation came later, for a landscape already emptied of its largest animals.

But the bones encode the atmosphere they breathed. δ¹³C in tooth enamel records the ratio
of C3 to C4 vegetation the animal consumed. C4 grasses dominate in warm, low-CO₂ grasslands.
C3 forests and shrubs dominate when CO₂ is high enough for C3 photosynthesis to be competitive
at altitude and in drier climates. The transition from C4 to C3 vegetation as CO₂ rises from
180 ppm (glacial floor) to 280 ppm (interglacial ceiling) is recorded in the enamel of every
animal that lived through it.

The bones are the receipt. The vegetation was the transaction. CO₂ was the currency.

The Páramo — one of Earth's fastest-speciation ecosystems, the high Andean grasslands — does
not exist primarily because the Andes rose. The mountains needed to be high enough. But the
Páramo exists where and when it does because CO₂ fell below a threshold during the
Pliocene-to-Pleistocene transition. [measured: EGU 2015, clumped isotope records]

Above ~400 ppm CO₂: C3 trees compete successfully at altitude; forests reached the highest
accessible elevations; the Páramo was compressed to thin ribbons.
Below ~280 ppm CO₂: C3 trees lose efficiency at altitude; the forest retreats down the slope;
the Páramo expands.
At ~180 ppm glacial floor: the Páramo expands further — and its own plants barely
photosynthesize, approaching the Rubisco floor.

Then the cycle reverses. CO₂ climbs. The forest advances up the slope. The Páramo is split
into fragments and reconnected. Split — reconnect — split. Each oscillation is a speciation
engine. 100,000-year cycles of geographic isolation followed by re-merger, running for
2.6 million years. That is why the Páramo has the diversity it does.

CO₂ is the architect. The mountain is the substrate. The oscillation is the tool.

The orbital forcing drives the ocean to pull CO₂ down. The Rubisco enzyme in terrestrial
plants sets the floor at which that pull stops working — approximately 180 ppm, where plants
begin releasing CO₂ rather than fixing it. Two different governing mechanisms. Two different
timescales. They have never directly met. They converge on the same number across 800,000
years of oscillation. [measured: *Nature Geoscience* 2025, clumped isotope LGM wood]

180 ppm is not a random equilibrium. It is the number at which the ocean's pump and the
plant enzyme are in exact opposition.
