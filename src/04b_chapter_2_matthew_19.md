## I. The Method

παραβολή means and is phonetically 'parable' through 'throwing alongside' — meaning 'juxtaposition' or 'comparison'. It is a form of rhetoric.

δὲ means 'on the other hand' exclusively through 'but' when argumentative, while it means 'it follows' through 'and' when narrative. "What happened."

Narrative tells events in sequence with causal/temporal succession.

Argumentative is atemporal; abstract premise δὲ conclusion. "What follows."

Arguments may encapsulate a narrative, and narratives may encapsulate an argument.

In parable, the narrative form is itself inherently argumentative; the argument is expressed in and through the narrative structure rather than alongside it.

λέγω is 'to say' (assert, argument), 'to speak' (recount, narrative), or 'to tell' (direct, parable) through 'to count' or 'to enumerate' then through 'to gather' which itself is through 'to lay together'.

Arguments assert gathered boundaries and constraints proclaiming the conclusion that follows. Narratives tell gathered details. Parables create narrative for the listener to derive boundaries and constraints.

λέκτρον means 'bed', while in plural form it means 'marriage bed'. A marriage bed is assumed to produce a child; it follows.

## II. Homeric Etymology of λέγω

Homer uses λέγω exactly twice. In both cases the subjects lay down with someone else physically which forms a union, but there is nuance.

Both required speech. One a request, the other deception.

Helen is the mortal daughter of Zeus. She speaks to Paris directing him to put her to bed, δὲ asserting that he is beloved by Zeus, δὲ arguing in encapsulated narrative that sleep will be pleasurable.

The daughter of Zeus commands that his beloved Paris put her to bed so that they together may take pleasure in sweet sleep, presumably in Odysseus's physical marriage bed.

The holy union of the body produces a child.

> λέξον νῦν με τάχιστα διοτρεφές, ὄφρα καὶ ἤδη ὕπνῳ ὕπο γλυκερῷ ταρπώμεθα κοιμηθέντες
>
> *"Put me to bed now, beloved of Zeus, so that we may lie down and take pleasure from sweet sleep"*
>
> Il. 24.635 — Helen to Paris

The other scene is more complicated. Hera lures Zeus to a bed of flowers on Mount Ida. She hopes the sex will lull him to sleep, but his vigilance prevails forcing intervention from Hypnos, who puts him in a coma. The act of laying down lowered his vigilance enough for Hypnos to control his mind.

Hypnos invokes the same word, but the union in reference is not Zeus and Hera on the bed of flowers. It is his unholy union — the invasion of Zeus's mind. The result: Achilles, son of a god, dies.

The unholy union of the mind produces the death of a child.

> ἤτοι ἐγὼ μὲν ἔλεξα Διὸς νόον αἰγιόχοιο νήδυμος ἀμφιχυθείς
>
> *"For I, yes, I lulled the mind of Zeus of the goat-skin, having wrapped him round with deep sleep"*
>
> Il. 14.252 — Sleep speaking to Hera

The terminus in the etymological chain of λέγω is 'to gather together' or 'to form a union'.

Etymologists of λέκτρον who dismiss this as heathen bed are reductive. Their reduction erodes a literal narrative in The Iliad.

## III. Verse 16 — The Question

**Greek:**
> Καὶ ἰδοὺ εἷς προσελθὼν αὐτῷ εἶπεν, Διδάσκαλε, τί ἀγαθὸν ποιήσω ἵνα σχῶ ζωὴν αἰώνιον;

**Standard translation:**
> "Good teacher, what good thing shall I do, that I may have eternal life?"

### Word-by-word

**Καὶ** — 'also', 'namely', 'as well as', 'like me as to you' → 'as one amongst you'

**ἰδοὺ** — 'lo!', 'behold!' — meant to grab attention.

**Καὶ ἰδοὺ** — 'namely [lo!]' — refers to the subject of the attention-grab.

**εἷς** — 'one' — the next verse defines this as authority.

**προσε** — 'having stepped forward' — an action prior to the main verb; acting before consequence; honorable.

**πρός** — 'face-to-face', 'in presence of'.

**-λθὼν** — dative-taking with πρός prefix, 'approaching', 'having come to' the subject.

**-λθὼν root** *h₁lewdʰ-* — originally 'to rise' or 'ascend' with an implication of latency. Connected to **λαθών** (a participle of λανθάνω, 'to escape notice', 'to do something without being seen') because etymology is often restrained by pedantry which conceals common sense.

**λαθών root λαθ- from** *leh₂dʰ-* — 'to be hidden', 'to cover'.

- *h₁lewdʰ-* carries a u-diphthong (-ew-). In zero-grade, the e disappears; the u remains. This is the movement of Emergence — the transition from hidden to visible, as a seed beneath soil.
- *leh₂dʰ-* has a laryngeal (h₂), forcing a pure 'a' sound into the Greek root (lath-), lacking any u or w. This is the state of Concealment — the shroud, the veil, the act of pulling back into shadow.

**-λθὼν for plural subjects** — 'those who have yet to ascend' (with latency implied)

**πρόςλθὼν** — 'to those who gathered in secrecy'

**προσελθὼν** — 'to those who have sworn the word and have yet [to ascend]'

**αὐτῷ** — dative singular of αὐτός, third-person pronoun; the speaker refers to themselves.

**εἶπεν** — 'I speak', ordinary verb for 'to say'.

**Διδάσκαλε** — 'a teacher', referring to 'those gathered in holy union'; but prior initiation denotes 'a pupil' — so both.

**τί** — 'who', 'which', 'what'.

**ἀγαθὸν** — 'good', from Aryan *gʰedʰ-* 'to unite'.

**ποιήσω** — 'I will do', 'I should perform', from Aryan *kʷey-* 'to pile up', 'to build', 'to arrange' — a planned union specifically in the sense of construction.

**ἵνα** — 'in order, so that' — referring to the planned union.

**σχῶ** — 'that I may hold', 'possess', 'receive', 'secure'. Aryan *seǵʰ-* 'to hold fast' → 'to adhere' → 'adherent' of the planned union.

**ζωὴν** — 'life', from Aryan *gʷeih₃-* 'to live', literally 'animate'.

**αἰώνιον** — 'eternal', from Aryan *h₂eyu-* 'vital force'.

*Animate* = 'to possess life', 'to fill with spirit and energy', connotation of 'soul'. So *animate vital force* = 'to fill with the eternal holy spirit'.

### Synthesized translation

> *One, in the presence of those gathered in holy union, I speak to those gathered as both teacher and pupil — uniting who with which and what I should arrange, so that my adherents are possessed with the eternal holy spirit.*

**Standard translation:**
> "Again I tell you, it is easier for a camel to go through a needle's eye, than for a rich man to enter into God's Kingdom."

### Word decoding

**πάλιν** — 'on the other hand' additively through 'again' from 'further'

**δὲ** — argumentative 'on the other hand'; narrative 'it follows'

**διὰ** — 'through' when pointing to space or time; 'because of' when pointing to cause

**τρυπήματος** — 'of a hole', 'of an eye', 'of a perforation'

**ῥαφίδος** — 'of a needle', 'of a sewing/surgical needle'

**ἀνά** — "up, upon, throughout, back, or again"; refers to Mars.

**διὰ τρυπήματος ῥαφίδος** — describes both 'a needle's perforation through space and time' (inverted reading of text) and the dual meaning of the 'eye' of a sac needle.

**ἐστίν** — third-person singular, present active indicative: 'is', 'it is'
