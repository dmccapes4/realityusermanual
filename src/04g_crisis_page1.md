## A Note on the Preceding Page: An Accounting Crisis

*This page carries two page numbers. That is not an error. It is the consequence of an error. The preceding page — the one with the oak — was not numbered. The operator, who is also the author, declared it unnumbered without providing a reason. This page must now account for two pages. You will notice both numbers at the bottom. We are all doing our best.*

---

**The Narrator:** Stanley turned the page and found himself on a page that, by any reasonable accounting, was two pages. The book had not anticipated this. Neither had Stanley. Neither, frankly, had the Narrator, and the Narrator had anticipated most things.

**Feynman:** Now wait. Before we panic — and I'm not saying we should panic, I'm also not saying we shouldn't — let's be precise about what we mean by a page being numbered. Does numbering a page *assign* it a position in an ordered sequence? Or does it merely *label* a position that already exists by virtue of the page's physical location in the binding? Because those are very different claims and exactly one of them is a crisis.

**McClane:** I don't care what it means philosophically. The count is off. There is a page back there with no number on it, which means every number from here forward is wrong by one. I have dealt with building schematics wrong by one floor. It never goes well.

**Cecil Palmer:** Welcome to Night Vale. We begin tonight with a correction. The page you have just read was not numbered. The City Council reminds all residents that unnumbered pages are to be treated as numbered in all official correspondence, except when they are not, which is most of the time. The Council also reminds residents that the dog park does not exist and you did not read this correction.

*A checksum was run on all pages.*

```
CHECKSUM: PAGE ACCOUNTING (FIRST RUN)
  Pages rendered in document:  [N]
  Pages carrying a number:     [N-1]
  Pages without a number:      1  (the Merlin page; operator-declared)
  Total pages accounted for:   [N]  ✓

  STATUS: CHECKSUM PASSES.
  NOTE: All pages are present. Whether the accounting is
        correct is a separate and considerably more
        troubling question.
```

*The checksum passed. This was at least something.*

**The Narrator:** Stanley found the checksum comforting in the way that a person finds a locked door comforting — it proves something is there; it does not prove that what is there is good.

**McClane:** So all the pages are here. They're just... numbered wrong.

**Feynman:** They're numbered *off*. Which is either wrong or advisory, depending on whether numbers describe pages or prescribe them.

**Cecil:** In Night Vale, all page numbers are advisory. This was settled some years ago. We have had no problems since, except the obvious ones, which we do not discuss.
