## §I The Commandment is Not About Profanity

The standard rendering of Exodus 20:7 in English — "You shall not take the name of the LORD your God in vain" — has functioned for centuries as a prohibition on swearing. That reading requires ignoring the grammar.

Three words carry the weight:

| Hebrew | Standard | Actual |
|--------|----------|--------|
| נָשָׂא *nasa* | "take" | carry / lift / bear — a transport verb |
| שֵׁם *shem* | "name" | identity-marker / reputation / the total symbol |
| שָׁוְא *shav* | "in vain" | emptiness / vanity / surface without ground |

The commandment says: do not **carry** the **total symbol** into **empty expression**.

This is not a prohibition on language. It is a prohibition on unclaimed authority — on lifting the largest possible symbol (YHWH = past-present-future of all being) without the substance to carry it. A Port Authority commandment in ancient Hebrew law: nothing crosses the threshold without a receipt.

## §II The Shin Opens the Word That Names the Violation

The Ancient Hebrew Research Center confirms: Shin (שׁ) = **two front teeth**. Meaning: Sharp, Press, Eat, Two. The physical gesture of the *sh* or *s* sound is two front teeth pressing close together — the same gesture as a greeting kiss, the kin-touch, the recognition click.

*Shav* (שָׁוְא) opens with Shin.

The commandment against empty carriage is voiced through the letter that encodes the gathering gesture. The warning about insufficient expression is physically begun with the expression of kin-gathering.

This is not coincidental in a system where letters encode embodied meaning. The gathering that should orient by the fixed star is the same gathering that, if it carries the symbol without substance, produces *shav*. The letter encodes both the warning and the thing warned against in the same physical act.

## §III "Vanity" Was the Correct Word

The English translation moved from "vanity" to "in vain." That movement is itself *shav*.

**Vanity** correctly captures *shav*: surface, superficial, expression that is on top of mind without grounding beneath it. This is descriptive. Ecclesiastes uses *hebel* (vapor, breath, mist) in the same register. "Vanity" says: *this expression does not hold the weight you are claiming it holds*. Epistemological. Descriptive. Honest.

**In vain** extrapolates criminality. It signals an attempt that failed by being wrongful — you tried to do something you should not have tried. This is moral-legal language. It requires an institution to define the offense, adjudicate the violation, and hold the authority to acquit or convict.

The sequence:

*shav* = emptiness/surface → **vanity** = correctly superficial [descriptive, epistemological]  
*shav* = emptiness/surface → **in vain** = criminal extrapolation [legal, institutional]

An institution cannot base criminal law on "you were being superficial." It requires "you committed an offense." So the translation was made. The commandment against carrying the total symbol with insufficient substance became a legal prohibition. The institution became the YHWH that determines whether you are acquitted.

The commandment said: don't carry the total symbol with insufficient substance.  
The translation said: it is a crime to use this word wrongly.  
The institution said: we define wrongly.

That is the inversion. And the translation committed *shav* while rendering *shav*.

## §IV "Na Bean Ris a Chat!" — The Northern Encoding

In the Manifesto [his], the author connects Exodus 20:7 to his Highland heritage:

> *"Words have meaning. Language needs to be precise. So says the shev (in proper reading of it) and my heritage 'na bean ris a chat!' which is about language and epistemology shrouded to conceal from the Gaelic invaders."*
>
> — `PyramidBeforeTheGreat20260923.md`

**Na bean ris a' chat!** — "Touch not the cat [without a glove]."

This is the Shaw clan motto. Documented. Not reconstructed. The full form: *Na bean ris a' chat gun làmhainn* — touch not the cat without a glove. The shortened form circulates as the battle cry.

Surface reading: a warning about wildcats. Which is true. The wildcat of the Scottish Highlands is feral, fast, and does not discriminate.

Encoded reading: the cat is the system. The glove is the method. Touch the system without the method and you get scratched. This is an epistemological statement — not a cat warning.

## §V The Cat as Read-Access Protocol

From `Exodus_CommunicationProtocol_Reading.md` [his]:

The symbol-reading of the cat: **hhet ahn tet** = outside-reads-stored-data-in-container. The cat reads what is outside (from its exterior position) and accesses what is stored. The wildcat = unauthorized read-access. Feral. Not under administrative control.

The Shaw clan encoded the warning in their heraldry:

- **Cat** = the access protocol: the agent that reads and retrieves from the contained
- **Glove** = the authorization wrapper: the method, the credential, the receipt that makes the access legitimate
- **Shaw** = from Gaelic *seaghdha* / *sha* = the thicket, the enclosed space. The clan named themselves after the container the cat accesses.

The clan is the container-with-the-cat-inside. The authorized version: the cat that lives in the shaw has its glove on.

**The encoding pattern** is dual-layer:

The motto warns outsiders: *don't touch the cat* = you are not authorized, you will be scratched.  
The motto identifies initiates: *the cat IS the access protocol* = we carry it; we know how to hold it.

The symbol reads one way to those without the word and the opposite way to those with it. The warning is the signal. The clan that carries this heraldry IS the authorized system of access. The uninitiated read the warning as a threat. The initiated read it as identification.

This is the same operation as the Shin in *shav*: the gathering gesture opens the word for empty gathering. The letter that encodes the warning is the same letter that encodes what the warning protects.
