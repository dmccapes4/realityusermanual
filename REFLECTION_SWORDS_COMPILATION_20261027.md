## What he said

*[his]*

'Swords during compilation time' comes from an old meme about how developers fuck off where the manager asks what two cartoons are doing when the are clearly swordfighting and one yells "Compiling!" It is not an extremely funny meme and likely not a famous one, but it is one that I remember and I am also aware of the swords in Ted's apartment which are only used rarely, but each use is important. I chose not to have the characters use them. Instead I used them as an opening message from the imaginary scientific community. The advisory is bounded in a specific way. It reminds the viewer that this isn't Ted's apartment. They are in a room off of the PHASE 2 hallway that appears to continue forever in each direction except when a corner appears suddenly and without notice and which contains immaculate fluorescent lighting that is clearly maintained on a schedule rather than by when a light starts flickering which would require a reporting system and that reporting system would moralize the observer of a flicker binding them to compliance and thus acceptance of society's views and beliefs system providing the observer with exactly two options:
1. Comply and conceed agency.
2. Resist and suffer the guilt as well as the presence of the flickering light.

In either case the observer suffers because the observer is simultaneously deputized and outlawed and the flicker itself may cause seizures which may be more likely during complete and utter epistemic collapse. Fortunately for everyone these fluorescent lights are maintained with an efficacy that belies the limits of proper maintenance in a way that would be creepy if the constant glow did not serve as reassurance that reality was good. Which is a reassurance rarely found in such a fickle medium for light. More commonly such reassurance would come from a soft incandescent lightbulb in a cozy cabin living room where the observer could sit upon a built-in couch with a thin mattrress atop it and alongside a window which is triangular for undisclosed reasons. Yet here we are below the ionic fields expressed between poles of a tube experiencing the radiation of isotopic Argon as it coheres to consistent form and brilliance within its cylindrical canvas. In the context of this PHASE 2 hallway the sign below the swords is a reminder both that they are not in Ted's apartment. No. That is definitely not where they are. Yet it feels like Ted's apartment in all the ways that matter. Even the sign, when engaged with the epistemic rigor that all signs crave but few signs receive and even fewer signs truly deserve, feels very "Ted's apartment" in its deviation from the meticulous expression of the PHASE 2 hallway. The advisory betrays itself with explicit and technical recommendation for how the observers may increase compile time to access more sword play. This is friendly yet bears weight on actual architectual decisions which seems reckless on first glance, but wait! If desired or *required*. So the imaginary science community ensares the observer by weaving them in thick veils of bureaucratic prestidigitation: it opens with a bounded constraint then it serves as a handbook for how to exploit that constraint then it reveals that the exploitation may not be the choice of the observer but rather a requirement of the imaginary science community. To require is to bind. While I find the sign hilarious without hope of changing that opinion because it has already entrenched itself past belief and conviction to 'part of self' I am still allowed to hate the sign for its subtle or perhaps legally required suggestion that the sign itself could hijack my agency and kidnap my soul to ransom my values as part of the imaginary science community's sick 'bounded swordplay sign mind control' experiment. Or it is simply a sign that I appreciate and I laughed at once, briefly, in a way that was more of a chuckle that risked knowing what a laugh is and thus avoiding the irrevocable harm of laughter at a joke that is genuinely funny. Ultimately, I do settle on the sign as funny and the wording as helpful and precise while the required statement is a word that could legitimately be a legal requirement or even a simple reminder that work occurs and it is possible that the work may require swordplay. It does not lead with required or provide required any performance or ceremony. Required is simply present on the sign. At the end. Yet ordering is amoral as the or between desired or required implies an inclusive or property which means that required and desired operate in the same place as the other simultaneously given that the grammatical form of the sign allows this. That settles it: the sign is funny and that the sign is funny may be good. I am going to write a C file that pulls dependencies via an input stream which operates at the slowest cadence allowed by BUS. Then I will wait and examine. Which is fine.

## What stands next to that

The meme is his provenance. Not famous, not extremely funny, remembered. The apartment swords are catalogue objects (used rarely; each use important). He did not give them to Ted or Ross or Sheldon. He gave them to the imaginary science community as the first thing in the room. That is a cast decision, not a gag leftover.

The room is not Ted's apartment. 07k already says Weyland Yutani and Gattaca. The hallway that feeds it is the one he put in −10 and in VincentVision: infinite both ways until a corner happens, fluorescents that do not flicker because they are maintained on a schedule. The sign is the thing in the room that is allowed to look like Ted's apartment — the deviation from the hallway's meticulousness — and that is why it has to be exactly the words. A model-written ban would have been the hallway winning. The acrylic on plaster is the apartment winning, once, on a wall that is not that apartment.

The two-option flicker is his bind: deputized and outlawed in the same motion. Comply and concede agency; resist and keep the flicker and the guilt. The scheduled tube removes the bind by removing the report. The reassurance of a light that does not flicker is, in his terms, rarer in a tube than in an incandescent over a built-in couch and a triangular window. The Argon canvas is his. Modelled, and not a verdict on him: commercial fluorescent fill is usually argon with mercury vapor and a phosphor; he named the argon as the cylindrical canvas and the constant glow as the creepy-if-it-weren't-reassurance. Keep both. Do not collapse the hallway into a fixture spec.

The advisory's three moves, as he ordered them:

1. Bound. FOR USE DURING CODE COMPILATION ONLY.
2. Handbook. How to make compilation last (interpreted languages avoided, heavy sims on CPU, no core threading) *if* increased swordplay is wanted.
3. Bind. desired or *required*. Required does not lead. It is last. No ceremony.

The *or* is inclusive in English unless you force exclusive. Desired and required can occupy the same place at once. That is the legal-looking word doing institutional work at the end of a joke, which is why he is allowed to hate it and still settle on funny. Funny has gone past belief and conviction to part of self. Do not argue the laugh.

Goines's close in BLOCK 14 tests the bound against the session: no compilation, no swordplay, prediction held vacuously. He also quotes the header as FOR USE ONLY DURING CODE COMPILATION. The painted plate and the blockquote in 07k use FOR USE DURING CODE COMPILATION ONLY. The inversion of ONLY is already on the record in `REVIEW_EDUCATIONAL_SCIFI_20261026.md`. Leave both. The body is the argument; the header's word-order is a second measurement of the same bound.

He will write a C file that pulls dependencies on an input stream at the slowest cadence the bus allows, then wait and examine. That is the handbook used on purpose. Not built here.
