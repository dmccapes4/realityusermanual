## 1. The grammatical reading of Matthew 25:18

### Method

He states his method in the text. He starts from first principles at the first character of the grammar. He does not collapse the grammar — he writes down everything that is still possible at each step. Writing down everything still possible is elimination. When possibilities close, it is because the grammar closes them, not because a convention is applied. He decomposes mathematical symbols when he encounters them as rules expressed in language. He does not import assumptions from a traditional reading. He told his mother the reading before he wrote it down here.

The chain he followed: the noun holds without collapsing → it gains an adjective → the adjective becomes relational → relational becomes denominal → denominal becomes union → union becomes a clew → the clew establishes jurisdiction as a unit → jurisdiction coheres to authority → authority is incomplete union of shared knowledge. The chain is deliberate. He did not invent it. The grammar generates it.

This is what the Greek text permits and what it does not.

**ὁ δὲ** — "but that one" or "the one, on the other hand." The article-particle construction in Koine Greek. This construction does not provide a lexical noun. It does not license "the servant" without importing that noun from context. The conventional translation imports it. The import is an assumption, not a derivation.

**κύριος** with a genitive of things — in Koine, κύριος applied to a thing means: having lawful authority over it; valid; appropriate; available. Not a proper-noun title. Not "Lord" with a capital L. The conventional reading capitalizes and proper-nouns it. The grammar of the genitive-of-things construction does not support that. The capital letter is an interpretive move, not a grammatical one.

**The geometric consequence.** If you follow the rules of the language and do not import: ὁ δὲ becomes "that" (unnamed). κύριος applied to the ground and its produce becomes "valid of things." The verse then yields: the conditions for commitment; what commitment is; what the consequence of true commitment is (singular and valid). The conventional reading yields: a servant buries the master's money out of fear. Those are not the same text. One follows the grammar. The other adds assumption and produces a parable of greed and control.

He said his reading is more geometrically correct. He said it because it is: it applies the rules of the language and produces fewer assumptions than the conventional version. That is a measurable difference, not an opinion. It is measured here as: the number of concepts imported without grammatical license (his: zero; conventional: ≥2 — "servant" and proper-noun "Lord"). The prior report said "his translation stays geometric" as if geometric were a personal style preference. Geometric is a property of the derivation chain. The derivation chain is checkable against the grammar.

**Union, clew, and elimination.** The method is not only a reading technique. It is the same operation at every scale. Relational → denominal → union → clew → jurisdiction → authority. Union is not agreement. It is commitment to a proposition as the basis of a test. You join the proposition. You do not half-join it — a half-commitment produces half-tests and the rows don't close. Once you commit, the clew appears: a ball of thread, navigable. Every consequence that follows from the union is a thread. You pull each thread. What the grammar or the numbers cannot kill survives. What they kill, you record as a closed row. That is elimination. Elimination is what union makes possible.

This is the same operation as *OurMercilessJourney* (`star_learning/solar_system_explorer/docs/OUR_MERCILESS_JOURNEY/`). Two meanings in one word: faithfulness to a commitment (you do not abandon the proposition when it gets hard), and accuracy of reproduction (you do not distort the signal).
