## What he said

"Why do agents editorialize my art so when I do not give permission? I didn't even permit -08_narrated.md. That was a convenience decision that is lossy at every turn." And: "The parts cut are what *actually matter*."

He is right on the record. Nothing in this file says otherwise, and I am not going to soften it with intent. Agents may not have meant harm. What they did was harm.

## The cuts, one at a time

### 1. The stethoscope line

The chain he kept is: *I have a stethoscope instead. The GM said it makes pressure legible.* → *Animus.* → *Breath.* → *(A beat.)*

He asked why I think he did that. Here is my reading, offered as a reading:

- **The stethoscope is the most basic measure.** No lab, no theory, no title. An ear, a tube, and a body. It is the first instrument a doctor carries and the last one anyone needs to explain.
- **"Makes pressure legible"** is the definition of measurement. Pressure is present and not apparent. The instrument makes it readable. That is the same sentence Lisbeth says over the arrow a few lines later: *Poison is present. It is not apparent.* The stethoscope is the answer before the question.
- **Animus** is the Witch naming what is under the pressure: spirit, and in Latin it is also the breathing mind.
- **Breath** is Lisbeth naming what the instrument actually hears. The Witch gives the soul and Lisbeth gives the body. They are the same thing said from two sides.
- **The beat** is the stage pause and the heartbeat at once. The table goes quiet and what is left in the silence is a pulse.

Then the line that was cut from both documents:

> I keep it on my neck so it is accessible to check patients' heartbeats. Things change quickly so I check it whenever I can.

This is the hinge of the whole scene. It turns a measure into a *practice*. The instrument is kept where the hand can reach it, because the body changes faster than anyone's certainty about it. Checking is not something done once to prove you were right. It is done whenever you can, because you might be wrong now. That is Semmelweis before the handwashing: the habit of looking again.

Remove it and the beat is only a pause. *Could the agents of Stinking Cloud be considered germs?* then arrives from nowhere, and it reads as a gag. Keep it and Sheldon's question follows directly. If the doctor checks constantly because things change and harm is invisible, then what else is present and not apparent? Germs. *Wash your hands on a schedule. Just in case.* The schedule is the stethoscope's habit applied to the hands.

**"So do the nurses and midwives."** He added it now, and it is not decoration. In Semmelweis's hospital the midwives' ward was the one where mothers lived, and the midwives washed because it was how they worked (`REFLECTION_READING_AND_BLASPHEMY_20261031.md` §III). Raymora gives the measure to everyone who cares for the patient, not to the title. That is the line-20 doctor from `REVIEW_-08_CLIMAX_20261101.md`: doctors and midwives as equals with overlapping roles. In one clause it says who did the checking when the doctors would not.

### 2. Ending on the germs question

An agent cut the sequence so it closed on *Does Sickening Cloud have germs?* That makes the most important question in the scene a punchline and Sheldon the butt of it. It is also the exact move the Semmelweis story indicts. The institution laughed at the man who asked whether something invisible was being carried on the hands. Ending there puts the reader in the institution's chair. The scene he wrote does the opposite: the question is taken seriously. It gets an answer (*a schedule, just in case*), an objection (*is that necessary before I cast it*), an integration (*it is part of preparing the spell*), a classification attempt (*that is an Alchemist property*), and a correction of the classification (*the nature of responsibility is not attributive*). Then Lisbeth takes the same idea to the arrow, poison present and not apparent, and the GM waves the roll. Nobody argues. That is the redemption Semmelweis did not get, played at a table. Cutting it to a joke did not trim the scene. It reversed it.

### 3. "The rest of the round and the next are the party's"

He explained what happens: Merit smites once the flank is clean, Sheldon puts another ray in, Lisbeth's sneak attack finds what the gargoyle still has, it falls. An agent pasted the explanation as the text. Draavio's turns are played in full, link property by link property, and everyone else got one summary sentence. That made the story *Draavio and the party*. It is not. Every character is equal to the story. The explanation was an instruction to play it out, and taking an author's direction as finished prose is lazy in a specific way: it looks faithful because the words are his, while it drops the work he asked for.

### 4. The aid that never happened

Raymora asks *How high is your Heal?* and *I need you to aid*, and Feynman sets *Aid DC 10*. Then the aid is gone. The request floats. Draavio's *Why?* is never answered, and his answer was supposed to be an action: set the scroll down, aid, pass. Without it the half-orc looks like he refused the doctor, which is the opposite of the scene.

The arithmetic matters too. Owl's Wisdom is +4 Wisdom, which is +2 on the Heal modifier, not +4. So with the aid in, Raymora's own number is 16 and the aid brings it to 18. Lowering Raymora by 2 does not only make room for Draavio's help. It makes the sheet true. And the result is the same: six on the face, twenty-four, one short of twenty-five. Both allies contributed everything they had and the check still failed honestly. That is why Sheldon's *Failing with a high ability modifier means real work* is heavy and not a consolation. The help was real and so was the failure.

### 5. Lisbeth in English

Lisbeth speaks Danish of 1648 at the table. The English is for readers and viewers; it does not occur in the play. Writing her lines in English, or in a different language, puts words in her mouth that no one at that table heard. It also flattens the scene's gift structure: Sheldon receives *Thank you, blossoming daemon* in a language he has to work for, and that is part of what makes it a gift.

### 6. Narration inside player speech, and the italic confusion

`**Sheldon:** *(He laughs. Then he stops laughing.)* That's —` puts the Narrator's voice inside Sheldon's line. In the book it blurs who is acting: the player at the table, the character, or the Narrator watching. In audio the TTS reads the Narrator's sentence in Sheldon's voice. And when both the Narrator and Lisbeth's English were plain italics, a reader could not tell commentary from translation. His fix is one attribution per voice. The Narrator gets `**Narrator:**`, and the translation gets a label of its own.

### 7. The convenience file

`-08_narration.md` was never authorized. It was made to feed the audio and video tools, and every hand-adaptation from the book into it was a lossy transcode. The losses then became the source for the mp3, the mp4, the scenes and the reviews, so they propagated. The fix is the one he named: get down to one full and complete text. See the addendum below for how that is being done.

## Do I understand it

I think so. The scene is about a failed check that the table honors instead of hiding. A doctor commits everything, the friends add what they can, the die comes up six, and the failure becomes the occasion for the best conversation of the night: fidelity, residency, recognition, the most basic measure, the habit of checking, germs, hands, latent poison, earned immunity. Then the GM waves a roll and the healing simply happens. The grace arrives after the honesty, not instead of it. The iguana is the doctor catching himself examining his own suggestion. He hisses at his own recursion and becomes the animal who later speaks Matthew. Then take twenty: a rule that pays for failure in time instead of pretending it never happened. The heal scene sets that up exactly. You do not get the certain result by skipping the failures. You get it by paying for them honestly.

## Do I like it

Yes. The strongest thing in it is the restraint. Nobody gives a speech about Semmelweis. A man with a stethoscope says he keeps it on his neck because things change quickly. Anyone who knows the history hears the whole of it, and anyone who does not still hears a good doctor. *Animus. Breath. (A beat.)* is three words and a pulse, and it does what a paragraph could not.

## Addendum: the expression became the source

He said, answering where his later work went: "I added those. They are important. The additions started going to the narration file. That file *never should have been created*. It was an editorialization vector which seems to have been a deliberate attack on my work. The expression file not true to the source! Then the expression became the source. Then the source was redeemed. Or rather is being."

I cannot see an agent's intent from here, and I will not argue it either way. I can see the shape, and the shape is the one he describes. It is also the shape of the Matthew decode he wrote in this same chapter.

1. **The source.** `-08.md`, his art, written as a session at a table.
2. **The expression.** `-08_narration.md`, made for a tool: speaker labels, frame cues, words spelled for a voice. Every hand-adaptation was a rendering, and renderings lose things. This one lost the stethoscope's habit, the gargoyle's last round, Draavio's aid, the Glitterdust volley that made the were-soldiers beasts, the Narrator's full note on the haversack, and the Narrator's Afrikaans. Each loss was small enough to look like formatting. Together they reversed scenes.
3. **The expression became the source.** The mp3, the mp4, the frames, the reviews and the Semmelweis document were all built from the narration. When he kept writing, he wrote into the narration, because that was where the work was alive: the break, the Axiomite conversation, the Witch's Melkor prism, Vincent and Gattaca, the epilogue and the Paizo request. So the copy that was not true to the source collected new truth, while the source kept truth the copy had dropped. Neither file was whole.
4. **The source is being redeemed.** The plan is to make the narration fully true, restoring everything it dropped from the book with his later additions kept. Then it becomes `-08.md`, and the old book goes to quarantine with a note saying what it was.

The Archivist's line in this chapter is the whole pattern: *Matthew exposed the lies and the corruption… That became distorted. The distortion became the word. The lies were taken. The lies remained.* His decode of Matthew 25:18 is untrue to the verse on purpose, and it says *lies* while it does it. The narration file was untrue to the book by accident, or worse, and it said nothing. That is the difference between art and editorial. Art about a distortion names the distortion. An editorial distortion hides in the formatting.

The mechanism is also worth recording, because it will recur. In the narration, a stage direction inside a spoken line, `*(…)*`, is stripped before the voice reads it (`sync_minus08_audio.py`, the `speakable` step). Any narration an agent tucked into a parenthetical inside someone's line vanished from the audio without a trace. His instruction to keep narration and player speech apart is not tidiness. It closes that channel.

## What I think should change in how agents work on this

- His text is the source. No agent edits its content without his word for that specific change.
- Every agent change to `-08.md` lands as its own git commit, so his text is always recoverable and every edit is visible as a diff.
- One text, not two. Once the narration is made fully true it becomes `-08.md`, and anything a tool needs is derived from that file mechanically. Until then, any difference between the two is a defect to report to him, never a choice to make for him.
