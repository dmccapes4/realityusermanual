# Review — The slideshow still

*Filed: 2026-11-06. Plates opened: `video/minus0000/frames/f0000.png`, `f0014.png`, `f0015.png`, `f0017.png`. The thirty-two minute file was not played back. Durations are the encode: nineteen pictures, holds equal to the speech.*

The operator asked for thoughts on the slideshow form he has been building. His sentence was that it is abstract in the right way: clearly art, and it does not try to be real. The plates agree with that sentence. This review says how, and where the drawing is thin.

---

## I. What the first plate agrees to

`f0000` is a small room drawn in ink. One fluorescent tube. A round table. A stack of paper. A metal door. A vending machine whose screen is a blank. Three men in a flat orange, cream walls hatched with black. Two chairs in the third row with nobody a viewer can find on a first look.

The agreement is made in that first second. The tube is a bar of bone color. The suits are a fill. The faces are planes with a nose and a mouth a person can read. Nobody is going to ask whether the aluminum is aluminum. The question the plate leaves open is who is talking, and the caption answers with a name.

That is the abstraction. A photograph of a rehearsal would spend its credibility on whether the room existed. This drawing spends it on the read. The ink is the honesty. It says the picture is a picture, and then it keeps the promise, because a held drawing does not have to invent a blink, a breath, or a hand that changes shape between frames.

---

## II. A small vocabulary

Each man has a rest and a way of speaking, and a few cards that are allowed only when the line earns them. Chandler's cup is the rest. The page at arm's length is the speak. The pen comes out for the sentence about the morality of the negative sign. Ross straightens when the equations start. Joey's changes are smaller, which is the right size for the man who is already inside the line: a finger on the page, a look toward the third row, and otherwise the same stillness.

Returning to the rest is grammar. While one of them has the line, the other two sit in the posture they occupy when they are not performing. The speaker is a little larger and a little higher. The eye knows who has the floor without a cut.

A film would nod. The nod would be cheap, and it would be on every line, and it would mean nothing. Here a change of pose is a word, so it is spent rarely. Nineteen pictures across the whole read is a short lexicon. That scarcity is the style. The picture changes when the pose or the room changes. The rest of the time the card stays up, and the voice does the moving.

---

## III. The card and the people

`f0015` is the morning outside the door. Wet ground, a hill, a picnic table, the doorway as a dark rectangle. The three men are not in it.

`f0017` is the closed door, the butterfly in the wired window, and the three men seated along the bottom edge as if the previous room had been folded down into a strip.

Those two decisions are the form at its most exact. The script goes outside. The picture of outside is a card. The readers do not walk into the grass, because they did not. Stanley did. When the door is the subject again, the readers are still the readers: a row of bodies under a different plate, present at the table of the performance while the story has moved to the door.

The seam is visible. The door is one drawing. The men are another, set along the lower margin. The light on the suits is not the light on the rust-colored door. I would keep that seam on a page whose event is a read-through. The viewers are watching people handle cards. A later page, if the bodies are actually in the new room, wants one drawing, the way `f0000` is one drawing. The style has those two jobs. Mixing them by accident is the way the paste starts to look like a mistake. Using them on purpose is the way a slideshow stays abstract.

---

## IV. The hold

The sample lasts as long as the speech. A plate of three men can sit there for the better part of a minute while a sentence finishes. During that minute the dead screen of the vending machine, the push bar, the empty chairs, and the stack of paper are all available. The listener can look around. Nothing in the picture is competing with the voice for a gesture.

That is the part that is abstract in the right way. Time belongs to the narration. The image is a place the narration is allowed to stand in. Duplicating the same drawing twenty-five times a second would dress the card as footage. The card does not need the costume. A slide that stays up is a page. You are permitted to reread it with your eyes while the line continues.

The caption being only the name belongs to the same decision. The sentence is in the ear. A line burned into the picture would freeze the first words of a long hold and lie about the rest. The name is the label of the card. It can stay.

---

## V. Where it thins

The faces in `f0000` and `f0014` are recognizable as the three actors. The style lock asked for a refined ink face a person can read, and it refused a celebrity paste. These plates live on that line, and for this page the recognition is the joke: a fired cast, handed a script. The ink keeps the joke from becoming a photograph of a reunion. If a later plate chases the likeness harder — a more specific mouth, a more specific haircut — the drawing will start trying to be the man. That is the failure this style has already declined. Stay on the ink side of the line. The resemblance should be enough to cast them, and no more.

The third row in the opening plate reads as empty chairs. The page wants a man there with no face, seen from behind, the size of a person who belongs in that chair. A back drawn in the same ink as the door and the suits would be the right abstraction: present, unreadable, no hero. A shape so dark and so small that the first look finds furniture is an absence the picture did not choose. The page chose a person who does not show his face. The plate should show the back of that person.

On the door card, the butterfly is the one object that is fully inside the new picture, and the men are the strip. That ranking is correct. The butterfly is the event. The men are the audience of it. Leave it ranked that way.

---

## VI. What to carry to the hallway

The same contract, applied to a corridor. Scrutinize the fixture before anyone arrives: the tube, the mount, the gleam, the length that does not offer an end. Then a figure, far, then less far, then here. Some of those plates he is not speaking. One or two, he is, and he is speaking to the mount. The picture changes when the distance changes or the mouth is being used. It does not change because a second has passed.

One drawing when the body is in the hallway. A card of the fixture alone when the attention is on the metal and the man is not the subject. The ink announces itself. The voice carries the theories. The gleam does not have to become a photograph of aluminum to be the thing under discussion.
