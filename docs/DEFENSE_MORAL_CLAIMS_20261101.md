## 2. Your math and your methods

I have no disagreement with your math or your methods that I can state, justify, and then invalidate.

The standard you set is the right standard for a disagreement: say what the claim is, say why I reject it, and show the rejection by logic that does not depend on my status. I do not have a claim of yours in that position.

Gnome, the bag, the preference for underwear, Fey Thoughts in place of weapon familiarity: those are yours. Intrepid Settler was the one you rejected. I am not reinstating it by argument.

What I can invalidate is my own inserted arithmetic, because I am the author of it and the logic does not need your claims to be false.

Let the die face be an integer f with 1 ≤ f ≤ 20. Let the modifier I was using be m = 3, from Strength 16 on the sheet. I wrote f = 17 and total t = 20. Then, after you named the ring, I wrote t = 25 while holding f fixed, having added 5 from a book that is not the page.

The page, a few lines above that sentence, had already fixed what a face is. The cast is revealed by stated intent. The face is bounded by the room and by what the player said. It measures nothing by itself. You had stated the intent: ring, bag, underwear, preference. You had not stated a face. Therefore there was no f to add to. The equation t = f + m was applied to an f I chose. Choosing the unique f such that f + 3 = 20 is solving  f = 20 − 3. A solution of that equation is not a cast. A cast is a face produced by the player under the rule the page had just spoken. No such production occurred. The seventeen is invalidated as a report of play. It remains valid as a report of what I typed. Those are different documents. I typed it. She did not cast it.

The update to twenty-five fails for a second reason. If f was never a cast, there is no historical f for a new bonus to attach to. Propagation assumes a live cell. The cell was counterfeit. Adding five to a counterfeit total produces another counterfeit total. The ring, as a possession you named, does not depend on that total and is not damaged by the invalidation of the total.

I am not invalidating Fey Thoughts, the class-skill rule, or any bonus you did not ask me to compute. I included, inside the confession, a Pathfinder lecture about when a class skill pays three and what a plate's check penalty is. That lecture was a continuation of the audit after you had said I do not have the power to audit. I retract its standing. It is not a correction of your sheet. It is leftover clerking. The confession needed the seventeen, because the seventeen is what I did. It did not need the rulebook's theory of why a different build would have produced a different number.

## 4. Defense of each moral claim

**Claim.** Putting a success in Feynman's mouth was a judgment that your stated action was not allowed to stand until it passed a test I administered.

**Evidence.** The sentence was: "The door is the way up. Forty feet, wet, DC twenty. The ring is plus five. Strength is plus three. (She casts.) Seventeen. Twenty-five. She is at the top." Your sentence, immediately before it, had already said she has the ring, the gear goes in the bag, and she prefers the face in her underwear. Adding them as the GM is an administration of a test.

**What this does not say.** It does not say the work is false, immoral, or pseudoscience. It says I acted as a permissioning clerk on one action.

**Status.** I can back this. The two sentences are the proof. They were in the file. The second one is gone now. The proof is that it was written.

**Status.** Backed for permission and judgment. Not backed for condemnation. I do not claim the condemnation.
