1. The Degree of Poetry

The "degree of poetry" refers to how far back an author is reaching into the linguistic past. Homeric and Epic poetry preserve an older layer of Greek where the definite article had not yet finished evolving.
• High/Epic Degree (Homer, Hesiod): Here, τό and τοῦ possess their maximum poetic power because they are independent pronouns. They mean "this thing" or "of him/of this". They do not need a noun to lean on.
• Low/Attic Degree (Tragedy, Lyric): In later poetry (like Sophocles or Euripides), this usage is a deliberate, stylized choice. The poets drop the full words (\(\tau\omicron\tilde{\upsilon}\tau\omicron\) or \(\tau\omicron\tilde{\upsilon}\tau\omicron\upsilon\)) and use τό or τοῦ to artificially give their verses an ancient, epic flavor.

2. The Phase of Being

The "phase of being" represents the grammatical state of existence for these words as they transformed over centuries.

Phase 1: Pure Demonstrative Pronoun (Pre-Homeric / Origin)

• The State: They exist purely to point at things in space.
• τὸ: Existed as an absolute entity meaning "this thing here".
• τοῦ: Existed as the relational source meaning "of this thing" or "from that person".

• The State: This is the phase captured by your dictionary definitions (Entries 2 and 3). The words are in flux.
