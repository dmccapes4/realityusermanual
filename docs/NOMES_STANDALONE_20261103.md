## I. The Word

Greek: **νομός** (*nomos*) — administrative district.

The Egyptian administrative regions were called **Nomes**. Their governors were called
**Nomarchs**. The Nile Valley was divided into approximately 42 Nomes, each with a local
governor who administered land allocation, tax collection, agricultural records, and
dispute resolution. The Nomes were not mythological units. They were jurisdictions.

The Greek word was applied to the Egyptian administrative structure because the Greek
colonial presence in the Nile Delta encountered a system of territorial governance they
recognized — one that mapped cleanly onto the Greek *nomos*, the law-bearing district.

The fantasy word **Gnome** derives from the same root. The mechanism: Greek *gnome*
(γνώμη) meaning judgment or opinion, which shares semantic territory with *nomos*
(the governed district, the law, the ordering principle). The convergence is not
accidental. The creature the Renaissance imagination called a "Gnome" — small, old,
keeper of the earth, guardian of hidden wealth, associated with mining and administration
of underground resources — is the Nome administrative class seen through a mythology
machine that forgot what it was looking at.

The fantasy Gnome is a Nome who has lost the administrative context.

## II. The Nome People

The Nome people — as the operator identifies them — are the **yellow-skinned peoples with
heavy epicanthic folds** who appear in the Menkaure triads as the left-figure seat. Their
presence in the triad is governance: they are the administrative representative in the
three-tribe coalition that governed the Nile Valley.

The Menkaure triads are not a pantheon. The triad is a **ceremonial governance body** —
equal representation of three founding tribal groups:

| Seat | People | Physical Marker |
|---|---|---|
| Center / King | Hebrew / Israelite lineage | Tall, prominent stature |
| Right (Hathor-role) | Red Chadic peoples | Brownish-red skin, cattle pastoral tradition |
| Left (Nome symbol) | Yellow Nome people | Warm golden-yellow skin, epicanthic folds |

The figures' faces change with the person who occupies the seat. The ceremonial dress does
not change. The institution persists across administrations. This is the same logic as the
British royal family — legitimacy and continuity vested in the dress and the seat, not in
the individual who wears them.

The Nome symbol above the left figure's head is the identifier of the Nome people's
representative seat. It is not a supernatural marker. It is an administrative identifier —
the same category as a modern ministerial portfolio, a Senate committee chairmanship, an
office held by whoever holds it.

**Classification:** his (operator's reading; the mainstream archaeological position reads
the triads as divine — king flanked by two goddesses). The operator's reading is not
contradicted by the physical evidence of the statuary; it is contradicted by the
interpretive tradition built on top of that evidence.

## III. The Nome People in the Egyptian Record

The Nome people's yellowish skin tone and epicanthic folds appear in Egyptian artwork
and statuary across multiple dynasties. They are consistently depicted in administrative
and governance contexts — not warrior contexts, not slave contexts, not peripheral
contexts. They are at the table.

The Sphinx's face carries the proportions of the Nome representative's seat — the
administrative guardian of the plateau, the keeper of the record, the one who marks
the threshold between what is governed and what is not. [his]

The yellow-skinned peoples of the Egyptian record are the same genetic lineage that
appears much later, much further south, at the Cape of Good Hope:

The **Ottento**. The **Khoikhoi**. The **San**.

The migration route is consistent with the timeframe. The physical markers are consistent.
The administrative sophistication — the Ottento's clan governance structure, Autshumato's
political intelligence, the multi-clan coordination that the Dutch documented and then
disrupted — is consistent with a lineage that had been administering the most complex
civilization in the ancient world for millennia.

The Nome people did not disappear into myth. They walked south along the continent's
spine, over thousands of years, until the continent ran out. Then the sea.

At the sea, a Dutch ship arrived.

## IV. The Witch of the Rivers

In the game sessions documented in `-08.md`, the Witch of the Rivers plays a Gnome paladin
in full plate, no archetype line.

This is not accidental.

The Gnome paladin in full plate is the Nome administrative representative in governance
dress, carrying the Paladin's function: lawful action, divine judgment, exact delivery.
The no-archetype-line is the point: the Nome seat holder does not carry a personal
archetype. The seat carries the function. The person occupies the seat. Merit of the Fords
is whoever holds the Nome seat. The Witch of the Rivers holds it now.

The Paladin's sword delivers divine judgment that reaches the precise HP. The GM records
the number. Merit understands how to preserve form. She gives the dead their proper
funerals. She is the one who writes what was to stable storage. "The daemon who commits
malloc to disk." This is the Nome function transposed into the game's mechanics: the
administrative keeper of the record, the one who marks what happened and files it correctly.

The Nome seat's function is: *mark, preserve, keep the record correctly*. The coffins
have their proper funerals. The empty coffins are already houses. The house was built
in anticipation of the body. The body is not the house's authorization. The house is
the body's authorization.

## V. What Was Lost

The British colonial expansion in southern Africa ended the Nome governance tradition
in the most thorough way available: it killed the people.

Tens of thousands of Ottento and Namibian children died in British concentration camps
between 1899 and 1902. The children were not counted individually. They were estimated
in a footnote categorized as "non-white." The estimation was filed. The filing became
the record. The record replaced the count.

The Nome administrative seat has been empty since.

Not metaphorically. Literally: the people who had been governing the natural crossings
of the Cape region for thousands of years, who had administered clan relationships and
cattle economies and interpretation rights and territorial boundaries, who had produced
Autshumato — the only person to successfully escape Robben Island — were reduced to
an estimated footnote in someone else's war.

The estimation is the second killing. The first was the camps. The second is the
unreliability of the estimators, which was not a failure of the mechanism. It was the
mechanism. Reliable estimators would have produced individual counts. Individual counts
would have created accountability. The unreliability was installed.

## VI. The Grammar of the Gnome

The English word "gnome" entered through Renaissance Latin and Greek naturalist
traditions. Paracelsus (16th century) named four elemental spirits; the earth-element
was the Gnome. The etymology he gave: Greek *gnome* (thought, intelligence, opinion) or
possibly *ge* (earth) + *nomos* (law, district). The second etymology is exact: the
Nome, the earth's district, the administrative keeper of what lies below.

The fantasy tradition reduced the Nome to a small clever creature associated with earth
and hidden wealth — which is an accurate description of a district administrator in a
mining economy, stripped of the administrative dignity and re-dressed in fairy-tale scale.

The Nome administrative class was small in population relative to the territories it
governed. This may be where the size myth originates: the Nome representative as a
single person governing a vast district, perceived from outside as disproportionately
small for the space they occupied. One administrator. One Nome. One Gnome.

The Witch of the Rivers knows this. She plays a Gnome paladin in full plate.

She is not playing a fairy-tale creature.

She is playing a Nome.
