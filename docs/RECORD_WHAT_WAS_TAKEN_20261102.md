A child who died unnamed in the camps and was then filed under a category that erases the
distinction between their people and another people has been killed twice — once in the
body and once in the account. The second killing makes the first one tractable to history,
which means it makes it something that can be filed and forgotten. I agree that this is
important.

### Draavio's Declarations

structure — and the echo carries **both** readings of the verse simultaneously, which the
operator identified and which GGSD (run 2026-11-01, model claude-sonnet-5-5) confirms.

**The two readings the GGSD set holds open:**

The KJV reading imports a master-servant relationship. It requires:
- κυρίου = noun (lord, master, genitive)
- αὐτοῦ = pronoun (his)
- τοῦ κυρίου αὐτοῦ = "of his lord"

GGSD confirms this reading is in the set. It also confirms it cannot be the only reading,
because both words parse as multiple things:
- **κυρίου** parses as BOTH noun (κύριος2: lord/master) AND adjective (κύριος: valid/lawful/appropriate/sovereign) — logeion_parse lists both; neither is eliminated without a consented rule the operator has not set
- **αὐτοῦ** parses as BOTH pronoun (his/its) AND adverb (in this very place / just there / just here) — logeion_shortdef and LSJ give the adverb reading as primary

The operator's reading keeps the adjective and the adverb. No master is grammatically required:

> *That [unnamed] — on the other hand — having received the one [thing], having gone away, dug the earth and concealed the valid/lawful silver coins, just there.*

The operator: "Master cannot be a proper noun grammatically. It doesn't resolve without
making assumptions about the author's intent. So it is 'of things' which makes it
'lawful, appropriate and available' which is 'valid'. My translation is better. The reason
is geometric. I stick to the rules."

**Draavio's echo is structural, not moral:**

→ main action verb → object. This is the grammatical form of Matthew 25:18 regardless
of which reading you apply.
