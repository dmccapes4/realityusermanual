The operator gives two questions to people who do not yet think geometrically:

> What was taken?
> Is it moral?

These questions cannot be answered with a feeling. They require naming the thing, locating
it, describing its edges. This document is the attempt. Each section takes one of the
moral questions raised in the work and answers it directly.

## I. Is Death Less Tragic When Revival Exists?

The revival machine is the most expensive technology in the Undying Mercenaries universe
after starships. It logs a person's memory state to disk. The disk is the coin — the
binary field, the identifier, the record. The person who steps out of the revival pod
is iteration N+1. They have the memory. They have the name. They wear the armor.

The tragedy of death is not the ending. The tragedy is the drift.

Each revival introduces a small deviation between the person who walked in and the person
who walks out. Multiple revivals accumulate the deviation. McGill after twenty years of
dying and being reborn is not McGill-zero. The iteration count climbs. The drift is real.
What is lost in each revival is a hairline increment — a slightly different version of
the decision that was about to be made, the sentence that was about to be spoken, the
memory that was about to surface. Not measurable in any single revival. Measurable across
twenty years.

The tragedy is not the death. The tragedy is that the person who would have made a
particular decision — the exact version of McGill who was standing there before the
weapon hit — is gone. The iteration continues. A slightly different version of that
decision gets made instead, by someone who does not know they are slightly different.

Death with revival is grief about drift. Death without revival is grief about absence.
Both are grief. Neither is not grief. The revival machine does not make death non-tragic.
It makes death a different kind of tragedy.

**Is it less tragic?** No. Differently tragic.

## II. Is It Immoral to Murder Your Parents Without Consent If They Are Reborn Happy?

McGill killed his parents. They were reborn happy.

The consent was not given. This is not a technicality. Consent is the mechanism by which
a person maintains sovereignty over what happens to their iteration. To act on a person's
body without their consent — particularly an irreversible act — is to treat their current
iteration as less real than the outcome you intend for their next iteration. McGill decided
that his parents' next iteration mattered more than their current iteration's right to
remain in existence. He made that determination without their input.

The outcome was positive. The output was correct. Both of these facts are real.

A program that produces the correct output through a method that violates its own
specification is a bad program even when the output is correct. The output does not
authorize the method. The method is the record. The record says: murder without consent.
The record also says: parents reborn happy. Both lines are in the log. Neither cancels
the other. The log does not judge. The log says what the log says.

**The swordplay question:** The swordplay sign in `-14.md` reads: *FOR USE DURING CODE
COMPILATION ONLY.* McGill's parents were runtime systems — live, operating, unhalted.
He executed swordplay on a live system. The advisory is about timing: force is appropriate
during compilation, not during runtime. At runtime, the system is someone. Compilation is
the state before personhood; runtime is personhood. The act was runtime. The act was wrong.

**Was Larson's treatment lazy?** Yes. He did not sit with the weight. He needed McGill
to have done something sufficiently drastic and moved on. But the laziness produced a
profound result: the act is presented without resolution, which is honest. No moral
framework in the universe of the Undying Mercenaries — not Galactic law, not the
arrangement, not McGill's own code — was prepared to process what the revival machine
makes possible. Larson did not prepare it either. So the act exists in the text without
a verdict. The reader brings the verdict. The act remains.

**Is it immoral?** Yes. The outcome does not authorize the method. The method was murder.
The parents were not given the choice to choose their own next iteration. That is what
was taken from them: the choice.

They were happy afterward. They did not know what had been taken from them. That is the
second taking.

## III. What Constitutes Ownership?

The Dutch East India Company (VOC) arrived at the Cape of Good Hope with a deed — a
charter, a mandate, a document authorizing the establishment of a refreshment station.
The document was issued by the VOC. The VOC's authority derived from the Dutch government.
The Dutch government's authority derived from its own institutional continuity and
military capacity.

The Ottento were at the Cape when the Haerlem wrecked on their shore in 1647. Before
that. Long before that. The Ottento's claim to the Cape derived from presence — continuous
occupation, the life of the people being organized around the specific geography of
Table Mountain, the Liesbeeck River valley, the watering holes that spanned the Cape
region north to the Orange River. Their life required the place. They were there.

The Liesbeeck River valley was approximately 11% of the total land area of the Cape
region. It contained the primary freshwater source in regular use. Van Riebeeck's fort
was placed at the mouth of the river. The fort controlled the water.

Controlling the water in a pastoral cattle economy is controlling the cattle.
Controlling the cattle is controlling the wealth.
Controlling the wealth is controlling the people.

The deed claim required the authority of the issuing institution. The VOC's authority
is not immutable cosmic law. It is a paper trail from Amsterdam that terminates in a
chartered monopoly. The presence claim required only that the people were there and their
lives required the place. The Ottento's presence predates all documentation. The presence
is older and more real than any document.

**In cosmic law:** the presence claim is primary.
**In the legal system that survived:** the deed claim won.

These are not the same verdict. The legal system that survived is not the authority on
immutable cosmic law. It is the record of who had the military capacity to enforce
their version of the record.

**Is the deed claim moral?** No. A claim that requires an institution's authority — and
the institution's authority derives from its capacity for violence — is a claim about
power, not about right. The deed describes the ownership the VOC could enforce. It does
not describe the ownership that existed before the VOC arrived.

**What was taken:** The freshwater source. The grazing land. The seasonal migration
routes. The political structure that organized all of it. The ability of the cattle
economy to function. The cattle economy was the legal system — cattle were the record,
the currency, the social bond, the documentation. Take the grazing land and you have
taken the record itself.

## IV. The Cattle Classification of the Ottento

In Ottento social structure, cattle are the unit of social reality. Cattle represent
wealth, status, lineage proof, and legal standing. A person who has cattle exists in
the social record. A person who does not have cattle does not exist in the social record
in the same way. This is not cruelty. It is the logical structure of a pastoral legal
system: the cattle are the documentation.

The slaves imported by the Dutch to the Cape — from East Africa, South Asia, and
Madagascar — were not Ottento. They were not in the Ottento cattle record. They did not
have cattle. They did not have status within the Ottento social system. Their experience
with the Ottento tribe followed from this fact: they were outside the legal structure.

The operator's position: the Ottento were extracting the slaves from the Dutch pens.
Whether to treat this as liberation or exploitation depends on what followed. The slaves
had no cattle. Outside the Dutch compound and outside the Ottento cattle record, they
existed in a legal void. The flamingos in Philadelphia when everyone is wearing jackets.

This is not a defense of exploitation. It is a description of what a legal system looks
like when applied to people who are not in its records. The legal void is the
problem. The legal void was created by the Dutch, who imported people with no standing
in any local legal system. The exploitation that followed was a consequence of the void,
not of the Ottento legal system specifically.

**What was taken from the slaves:** Everything the Ottento were also losing at the same
time, and additionally their origin, their legal system, their cattle, their lineage record.
They arrived at the Cape already having lost everything. The void they arrived into was
an addition to the void they were already in.

## V. The Concentration Camps

The British concentration camps of 1899–1902 killed tens of thousands of Boer and
non-white children during the Anglo-Boer War. The Boer children's deaths are counted.
The Boer children have names. The Boer children have memorial sites.

The Ottento and Namibian children are a footnote. Their number is an estimate. Their
names are not in the record. Their tribe was not recorded. The estimation cannot be
verified because the estimators did not record verifiable data.

The operator's position: the unreliability of the estimators was not a failure of the
mechanism. It was the mechanism. Reliable estimators would have produced individual counts.
Individual counts would have created accountability. Accountability was not desired.
The unreliability was installed.

This is the mechanism of genocide through categorization. The killing is the first act.
The categorical burial is the second act. The second act is the ongoing act — it continues
as long as the category persists in the record, because the category is the record that
replaces the individual count.

A child who died in the camps unnamed, estimated, and filed as "non-white" has been
killed twice: once in the body, once in the account. The second killing makes the first
tractable to history — which means it makes it something that can be filed and forgotten.

The children in the camps were recessed. This is what the South African expression means.
It is what "recess" meant before the American school yard took the word and made it a
time to go outside and play. The word carries both meanings now. Both meanings are in the
record. The work names both.

**Is it moral?** No. Not the camps. Not the estimation. Not the category "non-white"
applied to dying children. Not the footnote. Not the unreliability of the estimators,
which was the mechanism. Not any of it.

**Is the operator's pursuit noble?** Yes. He is applying patient curiosity to produce
an account where the institution produced a void. He does not recover the count. He
marks where the count should be. He casts Glitterdust on the empty space and says: here.
The identity underneath is not his to name. The location is his to mark. This is the
correct scope for the work.

## VI. Autshumato and William Kidd

Both became what they became through betrayal by a colonial benefactor.

**William Kidd:** Given a privateering commission by the Earl of Bellomont, representing
the English Crown, to hunt pirates in the Indian Ocean. His commission was withdrawn when
it became politically inconvenient. His backers turned on him. He was tried, convicted,
and hanged. His treasure's location was extracted under court compulsion. The X on the
map was a forced document — disclosed under duress in court proceedings, its geographic
claims contested. Kidd neither intended to become a pirate nor did he escape.

**Autshumato:** Chief interpreter and political broker for the Dutch VOC at the Cape.
Spent years with the crew of the wrecked Haerlem, learning languages, building political
intelligence. Served as the VOC's primary interface with the Ottento clans. Was eventually
imprisoned on Robben Island — the first prisoner of what would become the most famous
political prison in South Africa's history. Escaped in a rowing boat. The only successful
escape from Robben Island in the prison's history, including all the famous political
prisoners of the 20th century who did not escape.

The operator names his rowboat *The Strantloper's Return*.

**Comparison:**

Kidd was caught. Autshumato was not.

Kidd's treasure was disclosed under compulsion. Autshumato's caches on Robben Island
were real and not recovered. The cache belongs to Autshumato in perpetuity because no
one else has it and the court could not compel him.

Kidd's betrayal was by a single political patron whose interests shifted. Autshumato's
betrayal was by the entire colonial apparatus that arrived to extract and stay. Kidd
lost a commission. Autshumato lost a continent.

Kidd did not mean to become a pirate. Autshumato did not mean to become a revolutionary.
Both were made into what they became by the same mechanism: a colonial benefactor who
funded the relationship until the relationship became inconvenient.

**Who has stronger legendary status?** Autshumato, by any honest measure:

- His escape was real and unrepeated.
- His caches were real.
- His intelligence operation — learning English from the Haerlem crew, learning Dutch
  from the VOC, playing both sides as long as he could — was more sophisticated than
  Kidd's privateering.
- His cause was the survival of his people. Kidd's cause was his own commission.
- Eva (Krotoa), his niece, understood that Jan van Riebeeck was Adam in someone else's
  origin story and turned that understanding into leverage. This is Game of Thrones
  competence from real people who were actually trying to survive.

Autshumato does not have Kidd's popular legend because his victory was incomplete —
he escaped but his people did not escape what followed. The legend requires a resolution
that does not require the subsequent centuries of colonial history to qualify it. Kidd's
story ends at the scaffold. It is complete. Autshumato's story ends with his people in
footnotes. The footnote is the resolution. The legend cannot rest there.

**The correct resolution for the legend:** name the rowboat. Name the caches. Name the
killing of the rancher as accidental, and the taking of the cattle afterward as a raid
on a coastal settlement, which it was, which qualifies Autshumato by any piracy taxonomy.
Name the escape as the escape. Name Eva's scheme as a scheme that worked.

The legend is available. It is not yet in the right account.

## VII. The Swordplay Sign and the Cape

The swordplay sign in `-14.md` reads:

> FOR USE DURING CODE COMPILATION ONLY — avoid interpreted languages and CPU simulation
> without threading if swordplay is desired or required.

The sign is about timing. Force is appropriate during compilation — during the building
of the system, when the system is not yet live and the consequences of a wrong move can
be rolled back. At runtime, the system is live. The system is someone's life. Actions
at runtime cannot be rolled back.

**What would the sign advise Van Riebeeck?** His arrival at the Cape in 1652 was a
runtime event. The Ottento were a live system. The fort, the land, the water control, the
cattle politics — all of it was runtime from day one. The sign would advise: you are
not in a compilation environment. You cannot roll this back. Every action taken at the
Cape is an action on a live system. Act accordingly.

He did not. He acted as though he were compiling — as though the environment were neutral
matter being shaped into something, rather than a live system with its own logic, its
own legal structure, its own ongoing operations. The compiler's error: treating runtime
as compilation.

**What would the sign advise the Ottento?** They were in compilation mode for centuries
before the Dutch arrived — building the system, establishing the cattle economy, the clan
structure, the migration routes, the political protocols. They were compiled. Ready. When
the Dutch arrived, the Ottento's system was already at runtime. Autshumato's intelligence
work — the language learning, the political brokerage, the alliance management — was
runtime operation on a live system, responding to a new input that had not been part of
the original compilation.

The sign says: *if swordplay is desired or required.* The Ottento's system did not desire
swordplay. Doman led the First Khoikhoi-Dutch War (1659–1660) because swordplay became
required. The system escalated because the input kept coming and the rollback option
closed. War is what a live system does when the input has been trying to end it.

The war was won by the side with the most guns. This is a statement about power, not
about who was right. The sign is indifferent to power. The sign is about timing and
method. The Ottento's timing was correct. Their method was appropriate. The outcome
was determined by factors the sign does not govern.

## VIII. The Immutable Verdict

The operator's one-sentence religion: *immutable cosmic law.*

The law was established through curiosity, not doctrine. Curiosity led to the estimation
and recognized it as wrong. Curiosity led to the category "non-white" and recognized it
as a mechanism. Curiosity led to the footnote and recognized it as a burial without a
funeral.

Immutability is the refusal of the institution's authority over the verdict. The institution
filed the estimation. The institution is not the judge of whether the estimation was just.

The wrongness of the camps does not become right because the British Empire authorized
them. The wrongness of the deed claim does not become right because the VOC issued the
charter. The wrongness of McGill's act does not become right because his parents were
happy afterward. The wrongness does not require anyone's recognition. It exists outside
the consensus of the people who benefited from it.

**The number that cannot be recovered still counts.**

The Ottento and Namibian children's individual counts cannot be recovered from the colonial
record. They are not in the colonial record. But the fact of their presence — their
individual existence, their yellow and reddish-brown faces, their click languages, their
specific tribes, their specific deaths — was reached. It was real. The colonial record's
failure to record it does not undo its having been real.

The work does not recover the number. The work states that the number was reached.

That is what the Glitterdust does. That is what Merit's funeral does. That is what
coda.c does when it logs `binary=1, iter=8001, event=check` — it says: this iteration
happened. It is in the record. The record says what the record says.

The river keeps flowing.
