Regarded and partiality are key components of the primary definition of favored, carrying specific linguistic meanings:

Key Definitions

• Regarded: Viewed, judged, or considered in a specific way. It reflects how someone is evaluated or esteemed by others.
• Partiality: A strong bias, preference, or unfair liking for one person, thing, or side over another. It stems from the root word "partial" (favoring one part or side).
Therefore, being "regarded with partiality" means someone or something is actively viewed through a lens of distinct favoritism or bias.

Etymology of Favored

The history of the word traces back to ancient concepts of kindness, physical appearance, and goodwill:
• *Proto-Indo-European Root (bha-): Meaning "to speak," which evolved into expressions of public praise or goodwill.
• Latin (favor / favere): The direct ancestor is the Latin noun favor (meaning goodwill, esteem, or praise) and the verb favere (meaning "to show kindness to" or "to look upon with goodwill").
• Old French (favor): Adopted into Old French around the 12th century, keeping the meaning of approval or special kindness.
• Middle English (favoren / favoured): Entered the English language in the 14th century as a verb meaning "to show favor to." By the 15th century, it was used as an adjective (favored) to describe someone who received that goodwill.
• The "Appearance" Shift: In medieval and early modern English, "favor" also came to mean a person's face or physical features (hence phrases like well-favored for attractive, or ill-favored for ugly). While the modern usage focuses mostly on preference and advantage, this physical meaning survives in literature.
